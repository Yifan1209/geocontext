GeoContext: measuring how VLMs weigh a user-supplied location hint

One resource supporting two tasks over 109 sites in 30 cities and 5 models.

  GeoHint    open-ended localization given a hint that is true but coarse
             ("I took this near X"), with the hint's distance and its
             referenceability manipulated independently while the image is
             held fixed. 21,933 scored responses.

  GeoVerify  binary verification that a photo was taken within 150 m of a
             claimed place, scored with signal detection theory so that
             discrimination is separated from a model's willingness to say
             yes. 1,448 trials, 6,270 scored responses.

Findings. Reliance on the hint is flat along both manipulated axes while the
error that relying produces grows steadily with distance, so the cost of
relying scales and the reliance does not. Where the imagery is illegible the
median ratio of answer error to hint distance is 1.00 -- the answer is the
hint. Telling the model the user may be wrong is obeyed everywhere but pays
only where the image gives it something else to trust; a structured evidence
checklist pays nowhere. On GeoVerify no model reaches d' = 1 at the tolerance
a deployment needs, and ranking by accuracy would order the models wrongly
because they differ sharply in response bias.

Released: the 109 context ladders, every reference-point and image audit
decision with both the human and the model verdict, GeoVerify trials and raw
responses, scored GeoHint responses, and the analysis scripts behind each
claim in the paper. Image IDs and coordinates only, not Mapillary pixels.

Two measurement notes are documented in the code and the paper rather than
left implicit. Four faults in the geocoding metric are diagnosed in
geocontext/geocode.py; each produced plausible-looking numbers rather than
errors. And the regime comparison is corrected for the fact that the
no-context baseline both assigns a site to a regime and serves as the column
that comparison reads (analysis/regime_split_half.py), with every p-value
resampled at the site level rather than the response level
(analysis/cluster_inference.py).
